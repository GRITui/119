## 3. Tone Flags: Alignment with Owner's Brief

**Owner's original brief:** "warm, funny, a bit tired; no tragedy."

### 3.1 — Hospital / Collapse Ending (Ending 4)

**Current text (storyData.ts, lines 1340–1360):**

The ending centers on **medical trauma** — hospital gown, heart monitors, collapse. Thematically, it fulfills the brief's "warning" angle (don't ignore your body), but the *tone* is melancholic, not warm-funny. The phrase "Thank Buddha" + Mom's tears reads as **genuine tragedy**, not "a bit tired."

**Flag:** This ending contradicts the brief's surface tone, though it may be intentionally serious as a risk path.

**Owner decision:** Is Ending 4 meant to be the "wake-up call" (serious, but ultimately redemptive)? Or darker/more explicitly warning? Current version hovers between both.

**Status:** Not rewritten in this card — flagged for owner review before #6 ships.

### 3.2 — Ending 1: Burnout as Isolation

**Current text:** Ending 1 delivers the promised "corporate climber" arc, but the *tone* is noir depression, not the brief's "a bit tired." It reads more like a warning against ambition (tragedy implicit) than warm-funny.

**Alignment issue:** The brief says "funny" in at least one or two moments per scene; this ending has **zero levity**. No joke, no small moment of absurdity, no warmth — just cold silence and burnout.

**Status:** Flagged for tone review. Ending 1 may be *intended* to be dark (the cautionary path), but it contradicts the brief's "warm, funny" surface.

**Current text (cr_5–cr_7):** Chai's speech is **entirely serious**: toxic hustle culture delivered as threat. The brief calls for "funny" — which doesn't mean comedy-sketch, but rather sharp observation or a moment of absurdity. Chai's "family" metaphor is sobering, not warm-funny.

**Status:** This is thematic accuracy (corporate toxicity *should* feel suffocating), but it shifts the overall tone toward dark drama. The brief is not met by this scene alone.
